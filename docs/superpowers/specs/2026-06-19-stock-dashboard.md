To build an effective support tracker for **VnIndex** and stocks based on long-term investment values and UI/UX principles, you should focus on data that identifies fundamental value rather than short-term market "noise."

### 1. Key Keywords for Your Tracker
Based on the principles of **Benjamin Graham** and **Warren Buffett**, your website should be organized around these core concepts to help you stay rational:
*   **Margin of Safety (Biên độ an toàn):** The difference between a stock's intrinsic value and its market price.
*   **Intrinsic Value (Giá trị nội tại):** The real worth of a business based on its assets and earnings, independent of its stock price.
*   **Defensive Investor (Nhà đầu tư phòng vệ):** Someone focused on safety and freedom from bother.
*   **Speculation (Đầu cơ):** Betting on price movements rather than fundamental business value (this should be flagged as a risk).
*   **Earnings Yield:** The inverse of the P/E ratio, helping you compare stock returns to bond interest rates.

### 2. Core Indices and Values to Focus On
Instead of just tracking monthly price changes, focus on the following quantitative filters to minimize **cognitive load** and interaction cost:

*   **Financial Strength and Stability:**
    *   **Current Ratio:** Ensure current assets are at least **2 times** current liabilities.
    *   **Debt-to-Asset Ratio:** Long-term debt should not exceed **Net Working Capital** (Current assets minus total debt).
    *   **Earnings Stability:** Track if there has been **positive earnings** in each of the last 10 years.
*   **Valuation Ratios:**
    *   **P/E Ratio:** Graham suggests a price no higher than **15 times** the average earnings of the last three years.
    *   **Price-to-Book (P/B):** The price should not exceed **1.5 times** the book value.
    *   **The "Graham Multiplier":** The product of P/E and P/B should not exceed **22.5**.
*   **Dividend History:** Look for a record of **uninterrupted dividend payments** for at least the past 20 years.
*   **Long-term Growth:** Look for an increase of at least **one-third** in per-share earnings over the last ten years.

### 3. UI/UX Design Implementation
Apply these principles from the sources to make the tracker "easier" and more intuitive:

*   **Minimize Interaction Cost:** Keep related actions (like adding a stock to your "Favorites" list or viewing its "Financial Report") in close proximity to the stock name.
*   **Establish Visual Hierarchy:** Use **size and contrast** to highlight the most important data (like the **Margin of Safety**) while de-emphasizing secondary information like daily volume.
*   **Data Presentation:**
    *   Use **numerals** (e.g., 1,000) rather than words for financial figures to make them scannable.
    *   Be generous with **white space** between table rows to reduce visual clutter and help you focus on the data.
*   **Navigation and Search:**
    *   Use **Autocomplete/Autosuggest** for the stock search bar to help you find tickers quickly without remembering the full company name.
    *   Implement **Search Filters** to allow you to scope the market down to only those stocks meeting your "Graham criteria" (e.g., P/E < 15, P/B < 1.5).
*   **Activity Feeds:** Use an **Activity Feed** to track "timely information" such as new financial report releases or dividend announcements in a chronological timeline.

### 4. Comparison Logic
Your UI should allow for **Pairwise Comparison**. Just as the sources compare eight pairs of companies, your tracker should allow you to see two stocks side-by-side to compare their **P/E ratios, growth rates, and working capital**. This helps you avoid the "mental trap" of looking at a stock's price in isolation.

**Analogy for Understanding:**
Building this tracker is like building a **digital filter for a gold miner**. Instead of looking at every shovel-full of dirt (daily price fluctuations), your UI/UX should act as a series of sieves that catch only the "gold nuggets" (stocks with low debt, steady earnings, and a high margin of safety) while letting the "silt" (speculative hype) wash away.
